About this Module

Obstetric point-of-care ultrasound (O-POCUS) is a focused scan done at the bedside, often with a handheld probe linked to a phone or tablet, to answer specific clinical questions: How many babies are there? Is there a heartbeat? Which way is the baby lying? Where is the placenta? Is there enough fluid? How far along is the pregnancy?[cite: 4] It is usually done by the midwife, nurse, clinical officer or doctor caring for the woman[cite: 4].

Who it is for: Midwives, nurses, clinical officers, medical providers, emergency/rural clinicians, and O-POCUS trainers[cite: 4]. Self-study takes about 3 hours; facilitated sessions take 30 minutes[cite: 1, 4].

Unit 1. Introduction and Definitions

Artificial intelligence (AI) is software that performs tasks we normally associate with human intelligence, such as recognising patterns in images[cite: 7]. Almost all current imaging AI is machine learning and deep learning built from layered artificial neural networks[cite: 7].

  • Targeted-View AI: Operator finds and holds standard views; AI guides the view, checks quality, and places calipers[cite: 8].
  • Blind-Sweep AI: Operator follows a simple sweep pattern without view-finding; AI interprets the whole sweep video to give an estimate[cite: 8].

Unit 3. Risks and Effects on Learning

AI tools act like a "spotlight"—reporting only what they are programmed to evaluate[cite: 15]. Key risks include:

  • The Blind-Sweep Blind Spot: A gestational age number does not rule out twins, intrauterine fetal death, low-lying placenta, or fetal anomalies[cite: 15].
  • Automation Bias: The tendency to over-rely on automated suggestions even when they are incorrect[cite: 16].
Safety Rule ("Judge First, Then Reveal"): Always estimate clinically, answer the O-POCUS questions within your scope, and commit to your own evaluation before viewing or relying on the AI result[cite: 17].

Unit 4. Ethics, Privacy and Data Security

In alignment with WHO guidance on the ethics and governance of AI for health (2021), users must protect human autonomy, ensure transparency, maintain accountability, and secure handheld device data[cite: 18]. Patient scan images must never be transmitted via unapproved personal messaging or social media groups[cite: 19].

Unit 5. Clinical Integration and Documentation

Before relying on any tool, verify regulatory approval, scope of practice, and local referral pathways[cite: 21]. Documentation must record three separate items to support auditability and individual accountability[cite: 23]:

  1. AI Output: Device, app version, protocol, and confidence flags[cite: 23].
  2. Clinician's Assessment: Clinical GA (LMP, fundal height) and O-POCUS findings[cite: 23].
  3. Next Action: Dating used, clinical plan, and referral details[cite: 23].
